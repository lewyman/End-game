# Antidepressants NCLEX Guide — SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs & Serotonin Syndrome

Antidepressants are one of the most heavily tested drug classes on the NCLEX. The exam won't ask you to list every SSRI — but it *will* ask you what happens when a patient on an MAOI eats aged cheese, or which two antidepressants should never be combined without a washout period. This guide covers what you actually need to know.

## The Four Classes You Must Know

| Class | Mechanism | Key Drugs | NCLEX Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SSRI** | Blocks serotonin reuptake only | Fluoxetine, Sertraline, Citalopram, Escitalopram, Paroxetine | First-line for depression; serotonin syndrome risk; discontinuation syndrome |
| **SNRI** | Blocks serotonin + norepinephrine reuptake | Venlafaxine, Duloxetine | Depression + neuropathic pain; BP elevation; serotonin syndrome |
| **TCA** | Blocks serotonin + norepinephrine reuptake (non-selective) | Amitriptyline, Nortriptyline, Imipramine | **Cardiotoxic in overdose**; anticholinergic effects; narrow therapeutic index |
| **MAOI** | Inhibits monoamine oxidase (breaks down serotonin, NE, dopamine) | Phenelzine, Tranylcypromine, Selegiline | **Hypertensive crisis with tyramine**; washout period required; last-line |

## SSRIs — The First-Line Workhorses

SSRIs are first-line for major depression, anxiety disorders, OCD, and PTSD. They're safer in overdose than TCAs and have a more tolerable side effect profile than MAOIs.

### Key Drugs to Know

- **Fluoxetine (Prozac)** — Longest half-life (4–6 days). Less discontinuation syndrome. Watch for activation/agitation early in treatment.
- **Sertraline (Zoloft)** — Safer in cardiac patients. Good for anxiety disorders.
- **Citalopram (Celexa)** — **QT prolongation risk at doses > 40 mg/day**. Max dose is a big NCLEX point.
- **Escitalopram (Lexapro)** — The S-enantiomer of citalopram. Cleaner side effect profile.
- **Paroxetine (Paxil)** — Most anticholinergic of the SSRIs. Worst discontinuation syndrome. Avoid in pregnancy (Category D — fetal cardiac defects).

### Nursing Considerations

- **Onset:** Takes 2–4 weeks for therapeutic effect. Patients often feel worse before they feel better.
- **Suicide risk:** Young adults (18–24) may have increased suicidal ideation in the first weeks. Monitor closely.
- **Side effects:** Nausea, insomnia, sexual dysfunction (anorgasmia, decreased libido), weight gain (long-term)
- **Discontinuation syndrome:** Dizziness, flu-like symptoms, "brain zaps," anxiety. Taper slowly. This is a classic NCLEX question: "A patient who stopped fluoxetine abruptly 3 days ago reports dizziness and electric shock sensations. What should the nurse do?" → Recognize it as discontinuation syndrome.

## SNRIs — When Depression Comes With Pain

SNRIs block reuptake of both serotonin and norepinephrine. The added norepinephrine effect makes them useful for neuropathic pain conditions.

- **Venlafaxine (Effexor)** — Worst discontinuation syndrome in the class. Taper over weeks, not days. Can increase blood pressure at higher doses.
- **Duloxetine (Cymbalta)** — Also approved for diabetic peripheral neuropathy, fibromyalgia, and chronic musculoskeletal pain. Hepatotoxic — monitor LFTs.

### NCLEX Pearl

If you see a patient with depression + diabetic neuropathy, think duloxetine. If you see depression + severe anxiety + BP elevation, think venlafaxine.

## MAOIs — The High-Stakes Class

MAOIs irreversibly inhibit monoamine oxidase, the enzyme that breaks down serotonin, norepinephrine, and dopamine. They're reserved for treatment-resistant depression because of their serious drug and food interactions.

### The Tyramine Reaction (Hypertensive Crisis)

MAOIs prevent the breakdown of tyramine, an amino acid found in aged and fermented foods. When tyramine builds up, it triggers a massive release of norepinephrine → **hypertensive crisis** (BP can hit 220/120, stroke risk).

**Tyramine-Rich Foods (NCLEX favorites):**

- Aged cheeses (cheddar, blue cheese, parmesan) — NOT fresh cheese (cottage cheese, cream cheese, mozzarella are OK)
- Cured/fermented meats (salami, pepperoni, summer sausage)
- Fermented soy products (soy sauce, tofu, miso)
- Sauerkraut, kimchi
- Tap beer, red wine (especially Chianti)
- Overripe bananas, avocados
- Fava beans

**Symptoms of Hypertensive Crisis:** Severe occipital headache, stiff neck, nausea, vomiting, diaphoresis, dilated pupils, palpitations, chest pain.

**Treatment:** Phentolamine (IV) or sodium nitroprusside. NCLEX will also ask about immediate nursing action — assess vitals, maintain airway, prepare for IV antihypertensives.

### The Washout Period — Classic NCLEX Question

Switching from an MAOI to an SSRI (or vice versa) requires a **2-week washout period**. For fluoxetine specifically, a **5-week washout** is needed because of its long half-life.

"If a patient is currently taking phenelzine and the provider orders fluoxetine, what should the nurse do?" — **Question the order. Contact the provider. Do not administer.** This is a classic NCLEX "which patient should the nurse see first" scenario.

## Comparison Table: Antidepressants at a Glance

| Feature | SSRI | SNRI | TCA | MAOI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **First-line?** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes (pain) | ❌ Rarely | ❌ Last resort |
| **Overdose risk** | Low | Low–Moderate | **High** (cardiotoxic) | High |
| **Anticholinergic effects** | Low (Paroxetine: moderate) | Low | **High** | Low |
| **Sexual dysfunction** | High | High | Moderate | Low |
| **Weight gain** | Moderate (long-term) | Low | **High** | High |
| **Discontinuation syndrome** | Moderate (Paroxetine: severe) | Moderate–Severe (Venlafaxine: worst) | High | Moderate |

| **Diet restrictions** | None | None | None | **Yes — tyramine** |

## 🚨 Serotonin Syndrome — Must-Know for NCLEX

Serotonin syndrome is a life-threatening condition caused by excessive serotonergic activity — usually from combining medications that increase serotonin. It develops **rapidly, often within hours** of a dose change or new drug interaction.

### Mnemonic: "HARMED"

| Letter | Symptom |
|---|---|
| **H** | Hyperthermia |
| **A** | Autonomic instability (tachycardia, HTN, diaphoresis, dilated pupils) |
| **R** | Rigidity (muscle) |
| **M** | Mental status changes (agitation, confusion) |
| **E** | Elevated reflexes (hyperreflexia, clonus) |
| **D** | Diarrhea |

### Common Triggers

- SSRI + MAOI (most dangerous)
- SSRI + SNRI
- SSRI + St. John's Wort
- SSRI + Tramadol, Meperidine, Fentanyl
- SSRI + Linezolid (antibiotic with MAO activity)
- Any combination of serotonergic drugs

### NCLEX Question Pattern

*"A patient on fluoxetine 40 mg daily is prescribed tramadol for back pain. What is the nurse's priority action?"* → Contact the provider. This combination risks serotonin syndrome.

### Treatment

- **Discontinue** all serotonergic drugs immediately
- **Supportive care:** IV fluids, cooling, benzodiazepines for agitation
- **Cyproheptadine** — serotonin antagonist (antidote)
- Severe cases may require **paralysis and intubation**

## Serotonin Syndrome vs. Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)

This is a classic NCLEX comparison question:

| Feature | Serotonin Syndrome | NMS |
|---|---|---|
| **Cause** | Serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, MAOIs) | Antipsychotics (haloperidol, chlorpromazine) |
| **Onset** | Rapid (hours) | Gradual (days to weeks) |
| **Reflexes** | **Hyperreflexia, clonus** | Hyporeflexia |
| **Muscle tone** | Increased (rigidity) | "Lead pipe" rigidity |
| **Treatment** | Cyproheptadine, benzodiazepines | Dantrolene, bromocriptine |

**Key NCLEX differentiator:** Hyperreflexia and clonus → serotonin syndrome. "Lead pipe" rigidity and hyporeflexia → NMS.

## Nursing Interventions Across All Antidepressants

1. **Suicide risk assessment** before starting and at every follow-up — highest risk is in the first weeks of treatment
2. **Baseline vital signs** and weight before starting
3. **Screen for other serotonergic drugs** before adding a new antidepressant
4. **Monitor for activation** — some patients become agitated or restless in the first 1–2 weeks
5. **Teach about onset** — therapeutic effects take 2–4 weeks; full response may take 6–8 weeks
6. **Never stop abruptly** — teach tapering and discontinuation syndrome symptoms
7. **Pregnancy considerations** — Paroxetine is Category D. Most SSRIs are Category C. Untreated depression also carries risks.

## NCLEX Quick Quiz

**1.** A patient taking phenelzine (Nardil) reports eating pizza with pepperoni and aged parmesan at a party. The patient now has a severe headache, stiff neck, and BP 200/118. What is the priority nursing action?

*Answer: Maintain airway and prepare for IV antihypertensive administration. This is a tyramine-induced hypertensive crisis.*

**2.** A patient on fluoxetine 20 mg is started on sumatriptan for migraines. What should the nurse monitor for?

*Answer: Signs of serotonin syndrome — agitation, hyperreflexia, clonus, hyperthermia, autonomic instability.*

**3.** Which antidepressant is safest in a patient with cardiac conduction abnormalities?

*Answer: Sertraline (Zoloft) — it has the least effect on cardiac conduction among antidepressants. Avoid TCAs (cardiotoxic) and citalopram > 40 mg (QT prolongation).*

**4.** A patient is being switched from fluoxetine to phenelzine. How long should the nurse wait after stopping fluoxetine before starting phenelzine?

*Answer: 5 weeks. Fluoxetine has a long half-life (4–6 days for parent drug, up to 16 days for active metabolite). Standard MAOI washout is 2 weeks for other SSRIs, but fluoxetine requires 5 weeks.*

## Key Mnemonics — Print These

**SSRI Side Effects: "SAD FACE"**
- **S** — Sexual dysfunction
- **A** — Anxiety/Agitation (early)
- **D** — Diarrhea/GI upset
- **F** — Fatigue
- **A** — Anorexia (early, then weight gain)
- **C** — Cranial (headache)
- **E** — Emotional blunting

**Serotonin Syndrome: "HARMED"** (see above)

**MAOI Diet Restrictions: "No Cheese, No Problem"**
- Aged **C**heese
- **C**ured meats
- **C**hocolate (excessive amounts)
- **C**hanti wine / tap beer
- Also: fermented foods, soy sauce, fava beans, overripe fruit

## You've Got This

Antidepressants look intimidating because there are so many drug names, but the NCLEX only cares about a handful of high-stakes safety patterns: Don't mix MAOIs with SSRIs without a washout. Know what serotonin syndrome looks like. Know that TCAs kill in overdose. Know the tyramine foods.

If you can answer these five questions, you can answer any antidepressant question on the NCLEX:

1. Is it an MAOI? → Check for tyramine diet + washout period.
2. Is there a new serotonergic drug added? → Screen for serotonin syndrome.
3. Is it a TCA? → Check EKG (wide QRS = toxic). Assess for anticholinergic effects.
4. Did they stop abruptly? → Discontinuation syndrome. Taper.
5. Are they 18–24 and just starting? → Suicide risk monitoring.

That's the whole game. Now go crush it. 💊🧠
